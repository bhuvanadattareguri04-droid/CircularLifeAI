SMARTPHONES AND MOBILE DEVICES

Smartphones contain plastic, glass, aluminium, copper, circuit boards, and lithium-ion batteries.
A working or repairable smartphone should be repaired, reused, donated, or refurbished before considering recycling.
Non-working smartphones that cannot be repaired should be taken to an authorised e-waste recycling facility.
Remove any personal data before donating or recycling a smartphone.
Lithium-ion batteries inside smartphones require special handling and must not be placed in regular household waste or general recycling bins.

SWOLLEN, DAMAGED, OR HAZARDOUS SMARTPHONE BATTERIES

If a smartphone battery is swollen, bulging, leaking, smoking, overheating, or otherwise visibly damaged, the device must be treated as hazardous.
Do not attempt to open, puncture, press, crush, remove, dismantle, or repair a swollen or damaged battery yourself.
Do not place a device with a swollen or damaged battery near heat sources, open flames, or flammable materials.
Keep the device in a cool, open area away from other items.
Contact a professional e-waste facility, certified battery recycler, or hazardous-waste collection service.
Do not dispose of a damaged lithium-ion battery in household waste, general recycling, or a regular bin.

LAPTOPS AND COMPUTERS

Laptops contain plastics, metals, glass, circuit boards, cables, and lithium-ion batteries.
A working laptop can be repaired, upgraded, reused, donated, or refurbished.
A non-working laptop should be repaired if economically viable, or sent to an authorised e-waste recycling facility.
Wipe personal data securely before donating or recycling.
Laptop components such as RAM and storage drives may be reused in other devices if they are still functional.

SWOLLEN OR DAMAGED LAPTOP BATTERIES

A laptop battery that is swollen, bulging, leaking, punctured, smoking, or visibly damaged is hazardous.
Do not attempt to remove, open, puncture, compress, or disassemble a swollen or damaged laptop battery yourself.
Do not place the laptop near heat, flames, or flammable materials.
Take the laptop to a professional repair centre or an authorised e-waste and battery recycling facility.
Do not dispose of a damaged lithium-ion battery in household waste or a regular bin.

LITHIUM-ION BATTERIES (GENERAL)

Lithium-ion batteries are found in smartphones, laptops, tablets, power banks, cameras, electric vehicles, and many other devices.
They contain hazardous materials including lithium, cobalt, and electrolyte chemicals.
Used lithium-ion batteries must not be placed in regular household bins or general recycling streams.
Take used lithium-ion batteries to a battery recycling point, e-waste facility, or retailer that accepts batteries for recycling.

For batteries that are swollen, leaking, punctured, overheating, smoking, or otherwise damaged:
- Do not open, puncture, crush, dismantle, modify, burn, or attempt to repair or repurpose them.
- Do not place them near heat, flames, water, or flammable materials.
- Do not throw them in household waste, general recycling, or a regular bin.
- Keep them in a safe, cool, ventilated place away from flammable materials.
- Contact a certified battery recycling point, e-waste facility, or hazardous-waste collection service.

CHARGERS AND CABLES

Chargers and electronic cables contain copper conductors, plastic insulation, and electronic components.
A working and undamaged charger or cable can be reused or donated.
Chargers and cables with frayed insulation, exposed wires, burn marks, or visible damage must not be used as they present an electrical safety risk.
Damaged chargers and cables should be taken to an e-waste recycling facility rather than placed in regular waste.

TABLETS AND E-READERS

Tablets and e-readers contain similar materials to smartphones: plastic, glass, circuit boards, and lithium-ion batteries.
Working tablets can be repaired, reused, donated, or refurbished.
Non-working tablets should be taken to an authorised e-waste facility.
The same safety rules for lithium-ion batteries apply: swollen or damaged batteries are hazardous and must not be handled with DIY methods.

DESKTOP COMPUTERS AND MONITORS

Desktop computers contain metals, plastics, circuit boards, fans, cables, and power supplies.
Working components such as RAM, hard drives, graphics cards, and power supplies may be reused or sold.
Non-working desktop computers should be taken to an authorised e-waste recycling facility.
LCD and LED monitors contain electronics and may contain small amounts of hazardous material; take them to an e-waste facility.
Older CRT monitors contain hazardous lead and phosphor compounds and must not be opened or placed in household waste.

PRINTERS AND PERIPHERALS

Printers and peripherals contain plastics, metals, circuit boards, and sometimes ink or toner.
Ink cartridges can often be refilled or recycled through manufacturer or retailer take-back programmes.
Non-working printers and peripherals should be taken to an e-waste recycling facility.

PLASTIC ITEMS

Clean, undamaged plastic items such as containers and packaging can often be reused, repaired, or donated.
Check the recycling symbol and number on the plastic item to determine whether your local recycling system accepts it.
Plastic items that cannot be reused or recycled should be disposed of according to local waste-management rules.
Plastic containers should be cleaned before reuse or recycling where appropriate.

GLASS ITEMS

Undamaged glass bottles and containers can often be reused or recycled.
Check whether your local recycling system accepts the specific type of glass.
Broken glass should be wrapped safely before disposal to prevent injury.
Glass from electronics such as screens may require specialised recycling.

METAL ITEMS

Metals such as aluminium and steel are widely recyclable.
Metal items in good condition should preferably be repaired, reused, or donated.
Metal items containing electrical or hazardous components may require specialised recycling rather than standard metal recycling.
Aluminium cans and foil are commonly accepted in household recycling streams.

PAPER AND CARDBOARD

Clean, dry paper and cardboard are commonly accepted in household recycling streams.
Reuse paper and cardboard for packaging or other purposes before recycling them.
Paper contaminated with food, grease, or chemicals may not be accepted by recycling facilities and should be disposed of according to local rules.

TEXTILES AND CLOTHING

Clothing and textiles in good condition can be donated to charities, second-hand shops, or textile banks.
Worn or damaged clothing that cannot be donated may be accepted for textile recycling by some retailers and charities.
Avoid disposing of textiles in general household waste where alternatives exist.

FURNITURE

Furniture in good condition can be repaired, reused, sold, or donated.
Some councils and charities offer furniture collection and redistribution services.
Furniture made from wood, metal, and fabric may be partially recyclable, but check local guidelines.

GENERAL CIRCULAR ECONOMY PRINCIPLES

When deciding how to handle an old or unwanted item, consider these options in this preferred order:

1. Repair — restore the item to working condition so it continues to serve its original purpose.
2. Reuse — use the item again as-is or with minor cleaning.
3. Refurbish — restore and improve the item so it can serve someone else.
4. Donate — give the item to a charity, school, community group, or individual who can use it.
5. Repurpose — use the item for a different safe and practical purpose.
6. Recycle — recover the materials through an appropriate recycling facility.
7. Safe disposal — use professional or specialist disposal only when the item contains hazardous materials.

Extending the useful life of an item reduces the energy, water, and raw materials required to manufacture a replacement.

DONATION GUIDANCE

Items suitable for donation include: working electronics, clothing, furniture, books, toys, and household goods in reasonable condition.
Before donating electronics, wipe all personal data securely.
Check with the receiving organisation whether they accept the type of item you want to donate.
Charitable organisations, community groups, schools, repair cafes, and second-hand shops are common donation destinations.

REPAIR AND REFURBISHMENT

Many consumer electronics, appliances, and household items can be repaired by a qualified technician.
Repair cafes and community repair workshops offer free or low-cost repair services for many types of items.
Manufacturer and authorised repair centres can repair devices while preserving warranties where applicable.
Refurbished electronics are a sustainable alternative to buying new; consider refurbished options when purchasing.

E-WASTE AND ELECTRONIC RECYCLING

Electronic waste includes computers, phones, televisions, printers, circuit boards, chargers, cables, and other electronic equipment.
E-waste contains valuable recoverable materials including gold, silver, copper, and rare earth elements.
E-waste also contains hazardous materials including lead, mercury, cadmium, and brominated flame retardants.
E-waste must not be disposed of in regular household waste because it can contaminate soil and water.
Take e-waste to an authorised e-waste recycling facility, retailer take-back scheme, or council collection point.
Many electronics retailers offer take-back services for old devices when you buy a replacement.

SAFETY PRINCIPLE

Safety is always the first priority.
If an item contains an actively hazardous component — such as a swollen or damaged lithium-ion battery, exposed live electrical components, leaking or unknown chemicals, or pressurised containers — do not attempt DIY disassembly, repair, modification, or repurposing of that component.
Professional handling and appropriate recycling or disposal must be recommended instead.
Hazardous items should be taken to a certified e-waste facility, battery recycling point, or hazardous-waste collection service.
